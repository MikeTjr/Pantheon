Theological Center: Jesus Christ

This concordance is structured with Jesus Christ at its theological center — not as one figure among equals, but as the ontological ground from which all other figures derive their ultimate meaning. Colossians 1:16–17 makes an absolute claim: all things were created through him and for him, and in him all things hold together. This includes every divine name, every angelic hierarchy, every cosmic principle mapped in this database.

This does not flatten the historical data. Pagan deities, angelic orders, esoteric traditions, and occult systems are documented on their own terms, with their own source texts. But every tradition vector can be read through a Christo-centric lens: pagan deities as corrupted or partial perceptions of the one true source; angelic orders as servants of the same sovereign; esoteric traditions as groping toward what the Incarnation revealed plainly.

Genealogical Structure

This database tracks the biblical genealogical chain from Adam through the patriarchs to Jesus Christ — the spine of salvation history. Matthew 1 and Luke 3 give two genealogies that together establish both royal legitimacy (Davidic covenant) and full humanity (descent from Adam). Key nodes: Adam → Enoch → Noah → Abraham → David → Christ.

Each patriarch is cross-referenced with their appearances in other tradition tracks — Enoch in Enochian and Islamic literature, Abraham in Kabbalistic and Islamic correspondence, Noah across Enochian and Islamic prophecy. This genealogical thread is the red line running through the entire concordance.

Research Depth Rating

The Research Depth meter on each entry replaces a simple percentage. It communicates how thoroughly an entry has been documented across its tradition tracks:

Primary Source — Extensively attested in multiple primary texts with full tradition-vector coverage and cross-referenced relationships.
Secondary Source — Well-documented but missing one or more tradition vectors; some relationships need further sourcing.
Partial — Entry exists with basic data; further research needed for full coverage.

Methodology

Three principles govern every entry. Source transparency: every correspondence is tagged with the tradition and text making the claim — ancient syncretisms, modern scholarly parallels, and polemical equivalences are each marked differently. Contested claims are preserved, not resolved: where scholars disagree, both positions are stored and flagged. History of ideas, not advocacy: mapping Augustine's claim that Zeus is a demon does not endorse that theology; every tradition is treated with scholarly care.
